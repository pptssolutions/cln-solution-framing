      01

      What we heard from you

      The brief in your own words

      If Cln did everything else badly

      It has to prove cleaning happened, with geo-targeting and time-stamped photographs.

      Your answer when asked what Cln must do well. Everything here is scoped around that one sentence.

      Three other things shape the design.

      ERINND already runs the business

      Locations, service frequency, pricing, staffing, supply, billing rules and invoicing.

      Cln should work beyond Clean & Protect

      For other companies too, including smaller operators with no management software at all.

      Crews span more than one employer

      Crews working CPGS contracts include people employed by other agencies, not only your own staff.

      What has changed since November 2025

      The earlier scope described one company digitising its own staff on a system built from scratch. The business has moved since: ERINND now exists on Quickbase, crews span more than one employer, and there is a plan to resell. Nothing from November was priced or signed, so this is a fresh proposal rather than a variation of an existing one.

      02

      Terminology

      Four words this document uses precisely

      These terms have been used loosely across earlier documents, and two of them mean very different things. Settling them here prevents the wrong access model being built.

      ERINND
      Your existing operations system, built on the Quickbase platform. Holds locations, contracts, staffing, supply, billing and invoicing.
      Cln
      The product being built: a mobile app and a web portal. Not a company name.
      Third party / vendor Not the same as below
      A separate business that buys ERINND and Cln from CPGS. Has its own clients, work orders, standards and billing, and its own ERINND instance.
      Sub-contractor Not the same as above
      An agency whose staff work on CPGS's work orders and contracts. Not a separate instance: scoped users inside your own system, seeing only their own agency's work.
      One thing to confirm

      An earlier note listed third-party supervisor and administration users by name: Kevin, D'Andre, Matt, Tamika and LaToya. Under the definitions above we have read those as sub-contractor staff. If any of them belong to a business that would buy Cln in its own right, they sit somewhere quite different and we need to know before the access model is built.

      03

      How Cln and ERINND work together

      Which system holds what

      ERINND keeps running the business. Cln handles what happens in the field. The split is by type of information, not by who uses it.

      Stays in ERINND

      The business record

      On Quickbase, as today

      • Locations, square footage, service frequency
      • Contracts, pricing and billing rules
      • Staffing and pay rates
      • Supply and inventory levels
      • Invoicing and client reporting
      Cln reads Sites, contracts and the crew roster
      Cln writes back Completed, when, by whom, the score, any exception
      Held by Cln

      The field record

      Mobile app and web portal

      • Photographs and video evidence
      • Location and time of each check-in
      • Checklist responses as captured
      • Work queued offline, awaiting signal

      Cln then sends ERINND the results rather than the raw material: job completed, when, by whom, the score, and any exception raised. That is what ERINND needs in order to invoice against proof of service and report to your clients.

      Why the evidence sits in Cln

      Quickbase includes a fixed amount of attachment space with each plan, sized against seats rather than usage. Photographing every task at a single site generates in the order of tens of gigabytes a year, and several terabytes at the scale you are planning for. Holding the images in Cln avoids that entirely and keeps ERINND doing what it is good at.

      04

      How this supports reselling

      One product, configured per customer

      There is one Cln application, in one place in the app stores. It is not rebuilt for each customer. When someone signs in, Cln identifies which business they belong to and loads that business's settings.

      ConnectionWhich ERINND instance to talk to
      WordingThe terms used for work orders and sites
      TemplatesThe checklist templates
      AccessWho may do what

      That configuration is held as information rather than written into the software. So onboarding a new vendor is something your admin team does in an afternoon, not a request to us for a new version.

      The part that protects your licence cost

      Crew, leads, supervisors and clients reach ERINND through Cln rather than logging into Quickbase. Quickbase treats a service account as a single paid seat, so two hundred crew members through that connection cost no more than two. Only your own operations and administration people, who work inside ERINND directly, need Quickbase seats. Cln is what keeps the platform cost flat as headcount grows.

      One term to secure in writing

      This rests on one term we cannot confirm on your behalf: that users reached through the API need no individual Quickbase licence. It should be written into the partner agreement while that is still being negotiated, rather than relied upon afterwards.

      What is built now, and what waits

      Built into the pilot

      Foundations

      • Every record belongs to a business, from the first table
      • Permissions resolved per business, not globally
      • Wording and labels held as settings
      • One person able to belong to more than one business
      Later phase

      Product features

      • Self-service onboarding for new vendors
      • Subscription and billing management
      • Branding per vendor
      • Importing data from another platform
      • Reporting across several vendors at once

      The left column is cheap now and expensive to retrofit. The right column is the reverse: it can be added later without disturbing what already works.

      05

      Who uses what

      Six roles, and where administration lives

      Whether someone is employed by CPGS or by a sub-contracted agency is recorded against the person rather than built as a separate role. A sub-contractor supervisor uses the same portal as a CPGS supervisor, seeing only their own agency's work.

      Mobile app · in the field

      Crew Staff

      • Check in at site with location and time recorded
      • Work the checklist for the visit
      • Capture photo evidence against items
      • Works offline, syncs when signal returns
      • See and resolve tickets raised on their work

      Crew Lead

      • Everything Crew Staff can do, since leads also perform the work
      • See the whole crew's progress at the site
      • Reassign tasks across the crew, including no-shows
      • Complete the on-site check before leaving
      • Raise site-level issues not tied to one task

      Client

      Deliberately narrow
      • View proof of service for their own site
      • Raise a ticket with a photo
      • Rate the visit
      Web portal · in the office

      CPGS Supervisor

      • Oversight across sites and crews in their scope
      • Track work orders in progress and completed
      • Own escalations and SLA breaches
      • Sub-contractor supervisors get the same portal, scoped to their own agency

      Quality Manager

      • Review exceptions remotely: failures, low scores, disputes
      • Accept, or send back with a reason
      • See the evidence trail for any visit
      • Not reviewing every completed job; that is a later phase

      Client Relations

      • See what the client sees, before the client does
      • Respond to client-raised tickets
      • Pull proof of service for a site or period
      All administration stays in ERINND

      Creating work orders, managing checklist templates, onboarding people and maintaining contracts continue where they happen today. Cln does not duplicate them. This is a deliberate reduction against the November scope, which proposed a separate administration console with its own form builder.

      How work gets verified

      Two steps, deliberately unequal in size.

      1. 1 On site · every visit

        Crew Lead completes a short check

        Before leaving site: pass or fail against each item, plus an overall rating for the visit.

      2. 2 Remote · exceptions only

        Quality Manager reviews what needs it

        Only where something failed, scored low, or a client disputed it. Accept, or send back with a reason.

      Reviewing every completed job remotely would roughly double the verification build, and verification is already the centre of the pilot. Exception-based review gives the same assurance at a fraction of the scope.

      06

      Connecting to ERINND

      What we recommend for the pilot

      Cln is built against a defined data contract: a fixed way of reading site, contract and roster information, and of writing completions back. For the pilot, that contract is fed by scheduled transfer rather than live two-way calls.

      1. 1 From day one

        A defined data contract

        Cln is written against a stable interface for reads and write-backs.

      2. 2 During the pilot

        Scheduled transfer

        Not the manual export and import you do today. The contract is fed on a schedule.

      3. 3 After the pilot

        Live connection

        The software is written as though the connection were live, so moving to it is a configuration change rather than rework.

      Why not live from day one

      Live integration touches terms still being negotiated, namely rate limits and the partner agreement itself, along with an earlier understanding that a network upgrade would be required. None of that is within our control, and putting it on the critical path risks the pilot slipping for reasons unrelated to the build. Confirming live integration becomes something the pilot proves rather than something it waits for.

      One thing to test early

      Reading site and contract information is low volume and sits well inside Quickbase's published limits. If it works without obstruction you get live reads during the pilot, with results batched back.

      07

      Features that span every role

      The cross-cutting items from the gap analysis

      The prototype introduced capabilities that belong to no single role and were not in the November scope. Each is a scope decision in its own right. Our recommendation against each, with where it stands in the earlier documents.

      GPS and geofence verification on check-in

      In pilot
      Where it stands today
      Live in the prototype. Explicitly excluded from Phase 1 in the SOW and deferred to Phase 2.
      Why
      This is the pilot's whole purpose: proving cleaning happened. The November exclusion is superseded by your own answer that geo-targeting and time-stamped photos are the one thing the system must do well.

      Time-stamped photo proof and offline capture

      In pilot
      Where it stands today
      In both the SOW and the prototype.
      Why
      The core evidence chain. Sites include freezers, basements, loading docks and mechanical rooms, so capture has to survive loss of signal and sync afterwards.

      Site visibility scoping

      Corrected
      Where it stands today
      The prototype showed the technician all eleven organisation sites rather than the three assigned.
      Why
      Treated as a correction rather than a feature. Scoping by assignment is the same mechanism that keeps a sub-contractor supervisor to their own agency's work, so it is built once and used everywhere.

      SOP depth: numbered steps, named chemicals, PPE

      Partly in
      Where it stands today
      Prototype task detail carried numbered SOP steps, named chemicals, PPE requirements, dwell-time timers and before/after reference photos.
      Why
      Steps, chemicals and PPE are template content and cost little, so they stay. Dwell-time timers and before/after reference imagery are deferred, because both need content authored per property before they mean anything.

      Supply and inventory module

      Deferred
      Where it stands today
      A full module in the prototype: SKUs, par levels, low-stock alerts, request flow. Explicitly out of scope in SOW §7.
      Why
      ERINND already holds supply and inventory. Rebuilding it in Cln duplicates a system you already pay for, and it is not needed to prove a job was done.

      Two-way chat (“Message Cln”)

      Deferred
      Where it stands today
      A quick action in the prototype. Absent from the SOW.
      Why
      Messaging brings retention, moderation and response-time expectations with it. The pilot uses notifications and the ticket thread instead, which covers the operational need.

      Policy and SOP acknowledgement banners

      Deferred
      Where it stands today
      Mandatory acknowledgement prompts in the prototype. Absent from the SOW.
      Why
      Belongs with compliance and onboarding, which is not what the pilot is testing. Needs a policy versioning model behind it to be worth anything.

      Certification tracking and expiry

      Deferred
      Where it stands today
      Shown on the prototype profile alongside shifts, average score and on-time percentage.
      Why
      Workforce compliance rather than proof of service. It also needs a record per person maintained over time, which a three-month pilot cannot produce.

      Sub-contractor scorecard

      Deferred
      Where it stands today
      Present in the prototype; the gap analysis proposed a crew-scoped version for the Crew Lead.
      Why
      A scorecard needs history to say anything. Build the evidence first in the pilot, then the scoring that reads it. The data captured during the pilot is what makes it possible later.

      Deferred does not mean discarded. Each of these stays on the roadmap, and the evidence captured during the pilot is what makes several of them, the scorecard in particular, worth building at all.

      Questions the gap analysis left open

      Does the Crew Lead's on-site check duplicate the SOW's 1–5 scoring, or replace it?

      Replace it, with one combined method: pass or fail against each checklist item on site, plus an overall 1–5 for the visit. That reconciles the SOW's per-item scoring, the pass/fail logic in the November delivery plan, and what the prototype actually does.

      Does the Crew Lead gate supply requests, or only see them?

      Deferred with the supply module. If supply stays in ERINND for the pilot the question does not arise yet, and answering it properly needs the approval chain defined first.

      Who are Kevin, D'Andre, Matt, Tamika and LaToya?

      We have read them as sub-contractor staff, supervisors and back-office, working CPGS work orders. If any of them belong to a separate business that would buy Cln in its own right, tell us, because they then sit in a different place entirely.

      08

      What the pilot covers, and what it does not

      One site, one flow, end to end

      In the three months

      Proof of clean, end to end

      • One live site, with its real checklist and real crew
      • Check-in with location and time recorded
      • Checklist worked through on the phone, offline where needed
      • Photo evidence captured against items
      • On-site check by the Crew Lead before leaving
      • Remote review of exceptions by the Quality Manager
      • Tickets raised, owned and closed
      • Client sees proof of service for their site and can raise an issue
      • Results flowing back into ERINND
      • Resale-ready foundations beneath all of it
      Not in the three months

      Deliberately excluded

      • More than one site, or more than one business
      • Supply and inventory inside Cln
      • In-app messaging
      • Certifications, policy acknowledgements, scorecards
      • Administration screens duplicating ERINND
      • Self-service vendor onboarding, billing, branding
      • Client report manipulation, staffing and scheduling views
      • Dwell-time timers and reference imagery per property
      • Anything on the Lessen side of the business

      The right-hand column is the more important of the two. It is what makes three months achievable, and it is the list we will hold ourselves to.

      09

      What we need from you

      Dependencies, with owners

      These sit outside what we can do for you. The items that affect the date are the ones where a delay moves the pilot.

      Affects the pilot date 3 items

      Which named Quickbase partner programme, and its written terms

      The published channel programme describes systems integrators and service providers. Resale of a branded product under CPGS's own contract is a different arrangement and needs confirming in writing.

      Confirmation that end users reached through the API need no Quickbase seat

      Quickbase documents a service account as a single paid seat. Crew reaching ERINND through Cln should therefore cost nothing extra. This should be a term in the agreement, not a reassurance on a call.

      Pilot site confirmed, with access and a named point of contact

      Site type changes the build. A distribution centre makes offline sync central; a bank branch barely exercises it.

      Also needed from CPGS 5 items

      Published API rate limit, and whether the partner tier raises it

      The documented limit is 100 requests per 10 seconds per token. Relevant when a whole crew checks in within the same few minutes.

      Attachment storage entitlement and the cost of exceeding it

      Sets the ceiling on how much, if anything, can be stored in ERINND rather than in Cln.

      Whether any network restriction applies to CPGS's realm

      Quickbase's documented integration path is token-based over HTTPS. The earlier understanding that a VPN upgrade is required does not appear in their documentation and is most likely a different connection type. A short confirmation closes it.

      Checklist templates and the crew roster for the pilot site

      Cln renders templates; it does not invent them. We need the real checklist and the people who will use it.

      Client-borne platform accounts and costs

      Firebase, Apple and Google Play developer accounts, SMS gateway, domain, SSL and hosting, as already set out in the November scope.

      On the three months

      Nothing we have received states what is setting that date. We have scoped to fit it on the assumption that it reflects a preference rather than a commitment to one of your clients. If a fixed external date applies, tell us and we will re-scope against it rather than discover the constraint later.

      10

      How we will know the pilot worked

      And who gets to say so

      Four things, measured at one site across three months. None of them is a count of features delivered.

      Evidence completeness

      The proportion of completed jobs carrying location, time and photographs: the thing a client would ask to see.

      It survives the site

      Work captured with no signal, syncing afterwards without loss. Freezers, basements, loading docks and mechanical rooms are the test.

      Crews use it instead of paper

      Adoption without supervision, after a single training session.

      A dispute gets settled with it

      One occasion where a client questioned whether work was done, and the record answered it.

      We need one named person at the site

      Someone who will say plainly that the app is slower than paper, if that is what they find, and who is senior enough to be believed. Without that, three months produces polite agreement, the pilot is declared a success, and we learn nothing before the larger build. A name and a role, not a team.

      Decide now what happens if that person says it is not working. If nobody has authority to act on it, the pilot is a demonstration with a longer run time.
